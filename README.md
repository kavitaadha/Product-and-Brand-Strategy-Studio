# Product & Brand Strategy Studio - Teaching Assistant Portfolio

I served as a Teaching Assistant for Product & Brand Strategy and coached **8 teams** through end-to-end new product development:  
**customer segmentation, value proposition design, positioning, go-to-market choices, and pitch storytelling**.

This repository is a curated portfolio of the *outputs I mentored*—built to show how I make strategy **clear, decision-ready, and measurable**.

---

## My role (what I did across all teams)

- Coached teams weekly to translate research into **sharp strategic choices** (who, what, why us, why now).
- Pressure-tested assumptions and improved logic so recommendations were **defensible in front of stakeholders**.
- Strengthened executive communication: narrative flow, slide hierarchy, and “so-what” clarity.
- Helped teams define success metrics and launch considerations (KPIs, risks, mitigation, rollout).

## Studio Catalog - 8 Brand Concepts



### Apple - “Apple Ring”
A minimal wearable concept designed to extend Apple’s ecosystem into habit and health tracking.  
The team developed a clear target segment, a tight value proposition anchored in Apple’s design language, and a positioning story that fits Apple’s premium identity while staying everyday-use practical.

### Bose - “Sleep Headphone”
A sleep-focused audio concept built around wellness outcomes (rest, recovery, and personalization).  
The team framed the product promise, differentiated it from existing audio alternatives, and built a narrative that connects Bose’s sound authority to a new “night-time ritual” use case.

### Netflix - “Netflix One”
An expansion concept that positions Netflix as more than streaming—building a unified experience across high-engagement content types.  
The team defined target audiences, clarified the value exchange, and developed a go-to-market narrative that protects Netflix’s core brand while adding a premium, sticky extension.

### Tesla - “E-Bike System”
A mobility concept designed as an ecosystem experience (product + service + brand behavior), not just a vehicle.  
The team articulated why Tesla can credibly own this category, framed differentiation through safety and user experience, and created a launch logic consistent with Tesla’s innovation-first positioning.

### Fitbit - “FitChip”
A discreet, lightweight wearable concept focused on everyday wellness tracking with clear retention value.  
The team developed the segment logic, clarified the emotional and functional benefits, and positioned the concept as a “low-friction” entry point into personalized coaching and long-term habits.

### Microsoft - “Lingo Copilot”
A language-learning concept that blends productivity thinking with learning motivation (practice, feedback, community).  
The team built a platform-style story, mapped competitive differentiation, and shaped the product promise around Microsoft’s credibility in tools that scale with the user.

### Dyson - “AuraStrip”
A premium home-tech concept positioned as an experience-led product—where design, performance, and environment work together.  
The team aligned the concept to Dyson’s innovation equity and built a strategy narrative that feels high-end, functional, and emotionally resonant.

### Lululemon - Team Concept
A lifestyle performance concept developed around brand community, consistency, and identity-led habits.  
The team anchored the product story in Lululemon’s brand purpose, clarified the target user, and built a positioning narrative that feels “on brand” while still novel.
